As quantum technology advances towards practical applications, researchers are actively broadening the search for scalable material platforms. While there are many physical realizations of qubits, this thesis focuses on point defects in semiconductors, defect qubits, as they have proven long qubit lifetimes on the order of milliseconds at room temperature. All qubit systems have a characteristic relaxation time $T_1$, which describes how long an excited state survives before decaying toward thermal equilibrium and consequently bounds the achievable phase coherence time $T_2$. Therefore, the $T_1$ time is a vital component for properly understanding defect systems. The $T_1$ time in point defects is strongly dependent on the temperature because of the increased phonon occupation. In this thesis, we model the relaxation rate of two point defects; the well-studied \glsentryfull{nv} center in diamond and the novel \glsentryfull{clv} center in 4H-SiC, which displays telecom-band emission frequencies making it appropriate for fiber-optic communication applications. However, accurate modeling of defect spin dynamics is complicated by spin contamination in unrestricted DFT, an artifact addressed in this work. The $T_1$ relaxation time was derived from first- and second-order spin-phonon coupling via finite differences. We apply two methods for calculating the \glsfmttext{zfs}, one including all valence-band and defect spin orbitals, and one restricted to the unpaired defect electrons. The results show that there is a strong connection between spin contamination and symmetry-breaking phonon modes transforming as the $E$ \glsentryfull{irrep} of the $C_{3v}$ point group, suggesting that spin contamination artificially enhances the contribution of $E$-symmetric modes to the relaxation rate. It was found that at high temperatures the $T_1$ time is strongly dominated by two-phonon Raman processes, which supports previous research. The \glsentryshort{clv} center exhibits stronger spin-phonon coupling, especially in the low-energy region, giving rise to additional allowed transitions not present in the NV center, due to the lower phonon energies of 4H-SiC. Therefore, the \glsentryshort{clv} center has a shorter room-temperature $T_1$ time $\qty{58.7}{\micro\second}\leq T_1\leq \qty{2.1}{\milli\second}$ compared to the \glsentryshort{nv} center $\qty{7.5}{\milli\second}\leq T_1\leq \qty{45.5}{\milli\second}$. These results identify spin contamination as a significant source of error in defect spin-phonon modeling, and provide a framework for benchmarking $T_1$ in wide-bandgap qubit candidates.
